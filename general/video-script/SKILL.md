# Scripting a video

Read `~/.claude/voice-profile.md` first, then adjust: written voice and spoken
voice differ. Sentences get shorter. Clauses get fewer. Contractions increase
even for writers who avoid them on the page.

## Step 1: Length decides structure

Ask which format, because they are different jobs:

- **Under 60 seconds**: one idea, no introduction, no sign-off. The first second
  is the whole battle.
- **Five to fifteen minutes**: one question, three or four movements, a real
  payoff.

Ask what the viewer can do afterwards that they could not before. If there is no
answer, the video is a monologue and will lose people at ninety seconds.

## Step 2: The first five seconds

Write these last, and write six versions. The opening carries more weight than
everything after it combined.

It must do one of: state the surprising result, show the finished thing, name
the mistake the viewer is currently making, or ask a question they cannot answer
and want to.

**Never open with**: "Hey guys", "In this video I'm going to", "Before we get
started", "Don't forget to like and subscribe", or anything that describes the
video instead of starting it.

## Step 3: Structure for the drop-off

Viewers leave continuously, so front-load value and re-earn attention every
thirty to forty seconds with a change: a new claim, a demonstration, a
counter-example, a shift in pace.

For long-form:

1. **Hook**, five seconds, the result or the mistake
2. **Stakes**, fifteen seconds, why it matters, no throat-clearing
3. **Movements**, three or four, each one self-contained and each ending with a
   reason to keep watching
4. **Payoff**, the thing promised in the hook, delivered plainly
5. **One next step**, if any

## Step 4: Write it spoken

Read every line aloud as you write it. If you run out of breath, the sentence is
too long. If it sounds like an essay, rewrite it.

- Contractions throughout
- One clause per sentence, mostly
- Numbers spoken as they are said, not as they are written
- Mark visual cues in brackets, so the script is shootable rather than only
  readable

## Never

- Ask for subscriptions before delivering anything
- Summarise the video at the start
- "But first, a quick word about"
- Filler that exists to hit a runtime
- Claims of results the creator has not personally had

## Step 5: Hand back

Give the script with rough timings per section, the six hook options with your
recommendation, and a note of any claim that needs a source on screen.
