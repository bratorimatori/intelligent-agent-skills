# Writing to a list

Read `~/.claude/voice-profile.md` first.

An email is not a blog post with a subject line. Someone let you into their
inbox and can revoke it with one click. Write like that is true.

## Step 1: One job

Ask what this email is for, and accept only one answer: share something useful,
sell something specific, re-engage a cold list, or announce a change. An email
doing two jobs does neither, and the second job is usually the one that makes it
feel like marketing.

Then ask what the reader gets. If the honest answer is "nothing, I want them to
buy", say so and write it as a straightforward pitch rather than disguising it
as value. Disguised pitches are the reason people stop opening.

## Step 2: Subject and first line together

They are one unit. The preview text continues the subject, and most clients show
both.

- Specific beats clever. "Three tools I stopped using" beats "My latest thoughts"
- Lowercase and plain wording usually outperforms title case
- No "Re:" or "Fwd:" fakery
- No manufactured urgency unless the deadline is real
- Under fifty characters where possible, because phones truncate

Write five options and say which one you would send and why. Do not present ten
and make the user choose blind.

## Step 3: The body

Open at the point. The reader gave you one line before deciding.

Keep it shorter than feels right. Most list emails should be under three hundred
words. If it needs to be longer, it is probably an article with a link.

One link, or at most two pointing at the same thing. Every additional
destination reduces clicks on the one that matters.

Close with a single, concrete action. "Reply and tell me which one you use" gets
replies. "Let me know your thoughts!" does not.

## Never

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "Just checking in", "circling back", "quick question" as a subject when it is
  not quick and not a question
- A P.S. that repeats the call to action verbatim
- Fake personalisation beyond a first name
- Countdown urgency for a deadline that does not exist
- Three exclamation marks across one email

## Step 4: The legal and practical checks

Before handing back, confirm:

- There is an unsubscribe link, and it is easy to find rather than hidden
- A physical postal address is present, which commercial email requires in most
  jurisdictions
- The from-name matches what subscribers were welcomed by. An unfamiliar sender
  name collects spam complaints from people who genuinely did subscribe
- The reply-to is a mailbox that actually receives, and somebody reads it

## Step 5: Say what you would test

Name the one thing worth changing next time: subject specificity, length, the
single call to action. One variable, or you learn nothing.
