# Checking claims before they ship

The rule is simple and hard: never write a claim you have not checked. This
skill is the checking.

## Extract the claims first

Go through the draft and list every sentence that asserts something checkable: a
number, a date, a capability, a comparison, a superlative. Superlatives are the
most dangerous, because "the most read page" and "one of the most read pages"
look similar and only one of them needs evidence.

Then, for each one, name the source that would settle it. If you cannot name a
source, the claim is not checkable and should be cut or softened.

## Checking

**Go to the source, not to your memory.** Fetch the page, query the table, read
the file. A number you remember writing is not a verified number.

**Check that the source says what you think.** A dashboard showing 125 visits to
`/tools/codeium` does not support "the article about Codeium is our most read
page". Those are different pages. Attribute to the exact object measured.

**Watch what your own tooling can miss.** A search that returns nothing is not
proof of absence until you have confirmed the search could have found it. If you
grep for `gpt-image-[0-9]` and the page writes "GPT Image 2" with spaces, you
get zero hits and no warning. Before reporting an absence, test the check
against a case you know is present.

**Check the negative claims hardest.** "There is no disclaimer", "it serves no
OpenAI model", "nobody replied" are the claims most likely to be wrong and least
likely to be questioned, because absence looks like a safe thing to assert.

## Grade what survives

For each claim, mark it as: verified against a named source, softened to what
the source supports, or cut. Then apply those edits.

Prefer the smaller true number to the larger unverifiable one. A pitch that says
600 search visitors and survives scrutiny beats one that says 1,900 and invites
a question you cannot answer.

## Say what remains unchecked

Some claims cannot be verified in the time available. Ship them labelled rather
than laundered: "according to the vendor", "as of the date this was written". A
reader can discount a sourced claim. They cannot discount one that sounds
certain.

## If you find you already published something wrong

Correct it plainly, once, and move on. Say what was wrong and what is true now.
Do not narrate the process, and do not apologise more than once.
