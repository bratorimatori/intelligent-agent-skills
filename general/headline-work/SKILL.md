# Working on a title

Ten unranked options are not help. They move the decision back to the person who
asked for help with it. This skill generates, then scores, then recommends.

## Step 1: Read the piece

Not the topic. The actual piece, or at least its argument and its best specific.
A title written from a topic is a category label. A title written from the piece
promises the thing the piece delivers.

Ask where it will run, because the constraints differ sharply:

- **Medium and blogs**: 60 to 70 characters before truncation, subtitle available
- **YouTube**: about 60 characters, competing against thumbnails
- **Email subject**: under 50, no subtitle, preview text continues it
- **Search-led**: the phrase people actually type has to be in it

## Step 2: Generate across angles

Twelve, deliberately different, not twelve rewordings of one:

- **The specific number**: "I replaced four tools with one and lost a week"
- **The mistake**: "The pricing bug that made a paid tool sort as free"
- **The contrarian claim**: "Most AI coding tools are worse in week two"
- **The direct how**: "How to check a vendor price without trusting a directory"
- **The comparison**: "X versus Y, after six months of both"
- **The question the piece answers**, only if it genuinely answers it
- **The plain description**, which is often better than the clever ones

## Step 3: Score them

Rate each out of five on:

- **Specific** - could this title sit on a hundred other articles? If yes, one.
- **True** - does the piece deliver exactly this? A title that overpromises costs
  more than it earns, because the bounce teaches the platform to stop showing it.
- **Curious** - does it open a gap without being coy? Withholding the subject
  entirely is clickbait and reads as such.
- **Findable** - would anyone type these words?

Show the scores. The user disagreeing with a score is more useful than the user
disagreeing with a ranking, because it tells you which criterion they weight.

## Step 4: Recommend one

Name it, and say why in one sentence. Then name the runner-up and say what would
have to be true for it to win instead.

## Never

- All-caps words
- "You won't believe", "This one trick", "Nobody talks about"
- A number that is not in the piece: no "7 ways" for an article with four
- A title promising a result the piece does not deliver
- Colons stacked twice: "AI Tools: A Guide: What To Know"
